What you are trying to do, how much you need, how much of your own cash you can put in, and your state. Back comes every funding path open to you on paper, least of your own money first, with the down payment rules, the grants that are open today, and what each dollar costs.

SBA says it in one sentence: “SBA does not provide grants for starting and expanding a business.” On SBA’s main loan, called a 7(a) loan, a startup needs a 10% down payment (SBA calls it equity), and it does not have to be your own cash. A documented gift, a grant that cannot be taken back, a personal loan repaid from income outside the business, assets you already own, and a loan whose lender waits until the SBA loan is paid off (full standby) all count. Through September 30, 2027, veterans who will own at least 50% of a business they buy can cover the whole 10% with a seller note: the owner agrees to be paid that part later. SBA Express loans carry a $0 upfront fee for a business at least 51% owned by a veteran or spouse. How this tool works

the sources · what “open on paper” means · the $0 figure · refresh dates

The sources. Loan rules come from SBA’s own documents: SOP 50 10 8.1, which governs 7(a) and 504 loans given a loan number on or after October 1, 2026, the fiscal year 2027 fee notice, and the procedural notices listed in the “What changed” section. Kiva, crowdfunding, grant, and state rows each come from the program’s own page. Every row in your results shows its source link and the date we read it. A row marked “reported” comes from a secondary source and says so.

What “open on paper” means. The published rules allow a business like the one you described to apply. That is all it means. A lender does its own credit analysis and can require more than SBA does. The tool gives no approval odds, because no data supports one.

Buying a business. In that mode the running total shows your own cash needed. SBA still requires equity of 10% of the purchase, and the figure reads $0 when that equity can come from a source other than your savings, such as a seller note on full standby. A seller note is a debt to the seller that you repay after the SBA loan, and it depends on the seller agreeing to it.

The $0 figure. “Most you could raise with $0 of your own cash” adds the maximum of each loan whose program states no down payment requirement, counts each once, and stops at the amount you need. It leaves out grants, because winning one is never certain. It leaves out SBA Express and 7(a) loans, because their down payment is the lender’s call or a percentage. When your answers show a gift, outside income, or a seller note, the results say so in words and the number stays the same.

The down payment. The tool reads the amount you enter as the total project cost and as the loan size for fee math. A startup (in business one year or less) or a purchase needs equity of 10% on a 7(a) loan. The list of what counts is SBA’s. The note that the SOP’s startup section sets no cap on standby debt is our reading of the text, labeled that way.

Grants, ended programs, and states. A grant shows as open only while its deadline is ahead of today’s date. A program is named as ended only when its own site or a government page says so, and the quote and link are shown. A state that has not been researched says “not yet checked.” SBA fee math is shared with Veteran Founder Stack so the two tools never disagree.

Refresh dates. SBA rules and fees are re-read every October 1. The veteran flexibility for buying a business ends September 30, 2027. Grant rows are re-read as their deadlines pass. The tool does not prepare loan applications, recommend a lender, or give financial advice.
